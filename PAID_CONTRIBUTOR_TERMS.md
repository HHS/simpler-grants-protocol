# Paid contributor terms

_These are the terms a contributor agrees to when claiming a funded issue._

## 1. Eligibility

Paid contributions are open to United States persons aged eighteen or over. A United States person means a US citizen, or someone the IRS treats as a US resident for tax purposes, such as a green card holder.

Paid contributions are not available to current employees of the US Department of Health and Human Services, or to current employees, contractors or subcontractors of any organization holding a contract with the US Department of Health and Human Services.

Paid contributions are not available to current US federal employees.

Paid contributions are not available to any person or entity on a United States sanctions or export-control restricted-party list, including OFAC's Specially Designated Nationals list, or ordinarily resident in a comprehensively sanctioned jurisdiction.

Anyone, anywhere, may contribute to the project without an award. Contributions from people who cannot be paid receive the same review, the same standards and the same public credit as contributions that carry one.

## 2. How funded issues work

A contribution issue is work inside a CommonGrants repository, submitted as a pull request and merged by a maintainer. An implementation issue is a CommonGrants-compatible API over a grant source, or a plugin for a grant portal, built and maintained in the contributor's own repository.

A paid contribution is an offer for a defined outcome. It does not purchase a contributor's time, and it does not create an employment or contractor relationship.

Claiming an issue does not guarantee payment. For a contribution issue, payment follows a pull request that is merged and that satisfies the acceptance criteria published on the issue. For an implementation issue, payment follows both reviewers confirming that the published work satisfies those criteria. Maintainers determine whether those criteria are satisfied.

Award amounts and scopes are published on the issue before it can be claimed. Amounts are not negotiated, before or after the work is done. The acceptance criteria that apply are the ones on the issue when it is assigned. Any later change is agreed with the assignee on the issue.

### Claims

A contributor may hold one active claim at a time across the program, and an assigned implementation issue counts as that claim. Up to three contributors may claim a single contribution issue: the assignee and up to two others waiting in order. A contributor may sit in the queue on other funded issues while holding an active claim, but cannot take on a second claim before the first one ends.

### Deadlines

You have fourteen days from the day the issue is assigned to you to open a pull request that is ready for review. The program administrator checks in on the issue at day seven. If you need more time, you can ask for one seven-day extension before your deadline, or the program administrator may offer one at the check-in, as long as progress is visible. With an extension, you have twenty-one days. Once your pull request is ready for review, the deadline stops while the team reviews it. If you haven't opened a pull request by your deadline, your claim ends and the issue goes to the next person in line.

### When a claim is released

Where a claim is released, the next contributor in the queue is offered the issue and has seventy-two hours to accept or pass. If the next contributor does not respond within seventy-two hours, or already holds another claim, the offer passes to the next contributor in line and they leave that queue. If you can't finish the work, comment on the issue to let us know, and it will be offered to the next contributor in line.

### Implementation issues

Implementation issues are not first come, first served. Unlike a claim, a contributor comments with a proposal: the source or portal they want to build against, when they expect to have it working, where the code will live, the license it will carry, how they intend to keep it working after the award is paid, and whether their organization benefits from the work. The issue stays open for two weeks so more than one contributor has a chance to propose. The reviewers then compare the proposals and assign the issue to one contributor, with the reasoning recorded on the issue. After two weeks if only one proposal is submitted, the reviewers will review and either accept or reject the proposal based on the quality of the response. An implementation issue runs to a deadline agreed with the contributor when the issue is assigned, based on the schedule in their proposal and recorded on the issue. Sixty days is the default, and it may be extended once by thirty days. The contributor posts a progress update on the issue at least every thirty days. If thirty days pass without an update, the issue is taken back from the contributor and opened to new proposals.

### Who gets paid

The award goes to the assignee, who must be the author of the pull request or, for an implementation issue, a named contributor to the published repository. Where a contributor who does not hold the claim submits work for the same funded issue, that work may be reviewed and merged on its merits but carries no award.

Payment may be declined where a contribution is found to have been made in bad faith, to contain plagiarized or improperly licensed code, or to breach these terms. Where payment is declined, the reason is recorded on the issue.

## 3. Payment

Payment is sent through Tremendous following the merge or the reviewers' sign-off, normally within five business days. Contributors select their payout method from the options Tremendous offers.

Payment is made to the individual who authored the contribution. We do not pay a third party on a contributor's behalf. Payment is sent to the email address you give the program at commongrants-contributors@agile6.com after assignment, and contributors are responsible for providing correct payment details. A contributor who receives $2,000 or more in a calendar year is issued an IRS Form 1099-NEC at year end.

Cumulative payments of $2,000 or more to one contributor in a calendar year trigger United States tax reporting. A W-9 is collected before the payment that crosses that threshold. Contributors are responsible for their own tax obligations on amounts received. Where a required W-9 is not provided, payment may be held, and the program may apply backup withholding as required by IRS rules. The program may decline to assign a funded issue where the award would take a contributor's total for the calendar year to $2,000 or more.

If a payment fails or is rejected by the payment processor, the program administrator will contact the contributor to arrange an alternative payout method supported by Tremendous.

A payment that is not redeemed within thirty days is returned to the program. The contributor can ask for it to be sent again by emailing commongrants-contributors@agile6.com.

## 4. Intellectual property

Work contributed to a CommonGrants repository is dedicated to the public domain under CC0, the same as the rest of the repository (see [LICENSE.md](LICENSE.md)). Where the work lives in the contributor's own repository, the contributor licenses it themselves, under the open license named in their proposal. By submitting a contribution, a contributor confirms that the work is theirs to contribute and that it contains no material they lack the right to contribute.

Contributors retain no separate claim over work merged into a CommonGrants repository, beyond the repository's public domain dedication and the public attribution attached to the commit history. An implementation issue transfers nothing to us. The contributor keeps their work.

Contributors respond promptly to any maintainer question about the provenance of their contribution.

## 5. Work that benefits the contributor

A contributor may claim a funded issue from which their own organization benefits, provided the code is public under an open license and anyone is free to read, fork and adapt it. Running it may still require access to the platform it was built for. Work that stays inside one organization's own systems is not eligible.

Where a contributor has an interest in the outcome, the contributor must disclose it when claiming the issue, and that relationship is noted on the issue so that the record is complete.

Funding an implementation does not mean HHS or CommonGrants endorses the contributor, their organization, or their product.

## 6. Conduct

The project [code of conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) applies to all paid contribution activity, including issue comments and pull request discussion.

Repeatedly claiming and abandoning issues, or claiming issues in order to prevent others from working on them, ends eligibility to claim future funded issues.

Disagreements about program decisions are raised through the channels set out in these terms. Public pressure campaigns, social media escalation, or approaching staff working on CommonGrants outside those channels are not acceptable ways to resolve a dispute.

Breaches may result in a warning, a temporary suspension, or permanent removal from the program. Permanent removal forfeits any unpaid awards.

## 7. Disputes

Disputes are raised on the issue in the first instance, and most are resolved there. Where a dispute is not resolved on the issue, a contributor may raise it by emailing commongrants-contributors@agile6.com within fourteen days of the decision. The administrator acknowledges within five business days and responds within fourteen business days, recording the reasoning publicly on the issue.

## 8. Privacy and data

To administer a funded issue and issue payment we collect a contributor's GitHub username, an email address, confirmation of eligibility, and payment details handled by Tremendous. Where tax reporting is triggered, we also collect the information required on a W-9.

This information is used to administer the program and to meet tax and financial reporting obligations. It is held in the program ledger, which is internal to the delivery team, and is not published. Payment details are held by Tremendous under its own terms.

Contributor names and GitHub handles appear publicly in the repository through normal open source attribution, and appear in program communications only where the contributor has agreed.

A contributor may request the removal of their personal information from the program ledger. Records required for financial or tax reporting are kept for at least seven years. Commit history and public issue comments in the repository are part of the open source record and cannot be altered or removed.

## 9. Changes and termination

These terms may change. Changes are published in the repository with at least fourteen days' notice and do not apply retroactively to a claim that is already active.

The program may decline to post an issue, raise the award on an issue no one has claimed, or decline to fund any individual issue at its discretion, consistent with these terms.

The program may be paused or ended at any time. If that happens, active claims are honored: work that is submitted and accepted against a funded issue already assigned will be paid.

## 10. Acknowledgment

A contributor acknowledges these terms by opening their claim comment with `/claim` and including the following:

```md
/claim

- [x] I confirm that I am a United States person aged eighteen or over, that I meet the eligibility requirements in section 1 of the paid contributor terms, that I have read and I agree to the paid contributor terms, and that any work I submit is mine to contribute.

GitHub username: @[username]
Does your organization benefit from this work? If yes, how:
How I plan to approach this: [one line]
```

For an implementation issue, the contributor acknowledges these terms by including the same confirmation in their proposal.

Once assigned, the contributor emails commongrants-contributors@agile6.com with their GitHub username and a link to the issue, so the program can reach them privately about payment.

The program administrator confirms the claim by assigning the issue and recording the expiry date on it. Where more than one contributor claims a contribution issue, it goes to the first complete claim. An incomplete claim does not hold a place in the queue. If you start work before the issue is assigned to you, you do so at your own risk. If the issue goes to someone else, your work can still be reviewed and merged, but it won't be paid.
